# Appendix A: Glossary

This glossary defines terms as they are used in IRSVS. Where a term is defined by an authoritative source, the IRSVS usage is intended to be consistent with it.

## Terms

**Adversary emulation.** A test in which a team reproduces the tactics, techniques, and procedures of a realistic adversary against the organization's production defenses to measure detection and response. Includes red team and purple team exercises.

**After-action report.** The written output of an exercise, recording what was tested, what was observed, and the findings to be corrected.

**Alert.** A notification generated by a security tool or monitoring rule indicating that an event of potential security interest has occurred.

**Chain of custody.** A chronological record of who has held an item of evidence, when, and for what purpose, from collection to disposal.

**Containment.** Actions taken to stop an incident from spreading or causing further harm, such as isolating systems, disabling accounts, or blocking network traffic.

**Control plane.** The management interface of a cloud or SaaS platform through which resources, identities, and configuration are administered, as distinct from the workloads running on it.

**Coordinated eviction.** Executing containment and eradication against all known adversary footholds in a planned, simultaneous action, so that the adversary cannot respond to partial removal by re-establishing access.

**Critical service provider.** A supplier whose compromise or failure would materially affect the organization's operations or expose its sensitive data.

**CSIRT.** Computer Security Incident Response Team. A team that provides incident response services to a defined constituency. National and sector CSIRTs serve a country or industry.

**Declaration.** The explicit decision, by an authorized role, that an incident is underway and that the incident response plan is invoked.

**Emergency access credentials.** Accounts or credentials held in reserve for use when normal administrative access is unavailable or untrusted. Often called break-glass accounts.

**Ephemeral workload.** A compute resource, such as a container, serverless function, or auto-scaled instance, that is created and destroyed automatically and may not exist by the time an investigation begins.

**Eradication.** Removal of the adversary's presence and the means by which they gained and kept access, including malicious code, unauthorized accounts, persistence mechanisms, and exploited weaknesses.

**Event.** An observable occurrence in a system or network. Most events are benign.

**Evidence.** Any data, artifact, or record collected during an incident that supports findings about what occurred.

**Forensic readiness.** The state of having arranged in advance for the data, tools, procedures, and access needed to conduct an investigation.

**Governing body.** The board of directors or equivalent group with ultimate oversight of the organization.

**Holding statement.** A brief pre-approved public statement acknowledging an incident, for use before full facts are confirmed.

**Incident.** An event or series of events that actually or imminently jeopardizes the confidentiality, integrity, or availability of information or systems, or that violates security policy, and that requires a response. Each organization defines its own threshold under requirement 1.1.3.

**Incident contact list.** The maintained list of contact details for incident response roles, alternates, vendors, and external parties.

**Incident lead.** The single individual in command of the response to an incident at a given time. Also called incident commander or incident manager.

**Incident record.** The authoritative record of an incident, including its summary, timeline, affected assets, actions, decisions, and status.

**Incident response plan.** The documented description of how the organization reports, assesses, handles, and closes incidents, and who is responsible for each activity.

**Indicator of compromise (IOC).** An artifact, such as a file hash, domain, IP address, or registry key, whose presence suggests that a system has been compromised.

**Information sharing community.** A trusted group, such as an Information Sharing and Analysis Center (ISAC) or a CSIRT network, through which organizations exchange threat and incident information.

**Isolated recovery environment.** An environment separated from production in which systems can be restored and checked before being returned to service.

**Legal hold.** An instruction to preserve data that may be relevant to anticipated litigation or regulatory action, suspending normal deletion.

**Logging standard.** The organization's documented definition of which log sources, event types, and fields must be collected.

**Near miss.** An event that could have become an incident but did not, through chance or because a control worked late.

**Notification obligation.** A requirement under law, regulation, or contract to inform an authority, a customer, an affected individual, or another party about an incident.

**Order of volatility.** The principle that evidence should be collected starting with the data that will be lost soonest, such as memory and network state, before more persistent data such as disk contents.

**Out-of-band communication channel.** A means of communication that does not depend on the systems that may be compromised or unavailable during an incident.

**Persistence mechanism.** Any means by which an adversary maintains access across reboots, credential changes, or other interruptions.

**Playbook.** A documented set of steps and decision points for responding to a specific type of incident.

**Post-incident review.** A structured review held after an incident to establish what happened, what worked, and what should change. Also called a lessons-learned review or postmortem.

**Prioritized incident scenarios.** The incident types the organization has identified, from its risk assessment and threat intelligence, as most likely or most damaging (requirement 1.4.2).

**PSIRT.** Product Security Incident Response Team. A team that handles vulnerabilities and incidents in the products an organization makes.

**Recovery point objective (RPO).** The maximum acceptable amount of data loss, expressed as a period of time before the disruption.

**Recovery time objective (RTO).** The maximum acceptable time to restore a service after a disruption.

**Responder.** Any person, internal or external, acting in an assigned incident response role.

**Root cause.** The underlying condition that allowed an incident to occur, as distinct from the immediate trigger.

**Security-relevant logs.** Logs that record events needed to detect or investigate incidents, as defined by the organization's logging standard, including at minimum authentication, administrative activity, and security tool events.

**Severity.** The rating assigned to an incident that reflects its impact and determines the response it receives.

**Significant incident.** An incident at or above a severity threshold that the organization defines as requiring formal incident management, a dedicated recorder, a final report, and a post-incident review.

**Tabletop exercise.** A discussion-based exercise in which participants walk through their response to a scenario without operating real systems.

**Tactics, techniques, and procedures (TTPs).** The patterns of behavior an adversary uses, as catalogued in frameworks such as MITRE ATT&CK.

**Threat hunting.** Proactive searching for adversary activity that has not triggered an alert.

**Traffic Light Protocol (TLP).** A scheme maintained by FIRST for marking information with the audience it may be shared with.

**Triage.** The initial assessment of an alert or report to decide whether it is an incident and how urgent it is.

**Trust anchor.** A secret or key on which an identity system's security depends, such that its compromise allows an adversary to forge credentials. Examples include directory service ticket-signing keys and federation token-signing certificates.

**Workforce.** Employees, contractors, and others who work under the organization's direction and use its systems.
